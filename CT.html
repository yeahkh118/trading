01 • Direction & investment thesis

Executive Strategy

A disciplined, asset-light trading model that combines dependable agricultural volume with differentiated Cambodian-origin products. This is a planning framework, not a guarantee of sales or market access.

Planning horizon: 5 years
Business concept

From Cambodian origin to verified Chinese demand

Proposed business model. Operate as a Cambodia-based exporter and sourcing/trading coordinator. Buy from qualified farms, aggregators, processors and authorised specialist suppliers; contract pack, test and ship against buyer specifications. Start without a large owned processing plant; use audited third-party facilities and invest in processing only after contracted volume and margin justify it.

Core value proposition. Reliable origin, consistent specifications, traceable lots, transparent documentation, responsive bilingual coordination and predictable fulfilment—not simply the lowest purchase price.

Target customers. Chinese industrial raw-material buyers, food ingredient distributors, nut processors, spice importers, premium food distributors and approved specialty-product importers.

Strategic priorities

The portfolio has different economics and compliance profiles. Sequence the launch rather than treating every product as equally ready.

Priority 1 • Anchor volume

Dried cassava chips

Build the industrial-buyer pipeline and sourcing logistics around verified demand, quality specifications, moisture control, loading performance and transparent price formulas. Take volume only when shipment economics and buyer payment security are acceptable.

Priority 2 • Quality-led

Cashew nuts & cassava flour

Use sample approval and specification-based trading. Clarify raw-in-shell versus kernel, food-grade versus industrial grade, and cassava flour versus cassava starch before quoting or declaring HS codes.

Priority 3 • Gated premium

Kampot Pepper & bird's nest

Protect authenticity and supplier credibility. Kampot Pepper must have legitimate GI-linked sourcing and claims. Bird's nest needs a product- and establishment-specific China eligibility check before purchase commitments.

Demand signals and what they mean

Opportunity

Cassava chips: China is a very large importer of manioc under HS 071410. Cambodia's reported share in China's 2025 data was much smaller than the market total—about US$890,100 and 420,000 kg under this line—so the opportunity is to win qualified contracts and scale fulfilment, not to assume market share automatically.

Industrial demand signal: Cambodia's Ministry of Commerce reported that an August 2025 MoU with COFCO Biotechnology covered 500,000 tonnes of dried cassava chips for China during 2025–2026, and that facilitated commitments exceeded 700,000 tonnes in less than six months. This is a commitment/market signal, not proof that the whole amount shipped or is available to a new exporter.

Cassava flour: China's starch import market signals ingredient demand, but flour and starch are not interchangeable customs products. Map each SKU to composition, production process, intended use and the correct Chinese HS code before using the starch statistics in a sales forecast.

Cashews: China reported imports of US$217.2 million under HS 080130 in 2025; Vietnam dominated this tariff line. Cambodia is present as a supplier, but the business should compete on kernel/shell form clarity, grading, aflatoxin and moisture management, supply assurance and verified landed cost.

SWOT analysis

Strengths

  • Direct access to Cambodian agricultural sourcing networks and local origin relationships.
  • Portfolio spans volume commodities and differentiated products, providing multiple routes to revenue.
  • Proximity to China and regional freight corridors can support buyer communication and replenishment—subject to route, season and border conditions.
  • Opportunity to provide Chinese-language service, supplier coordination and consolidated shipment management.

Weaknesses

  • Early-stage trading business may have limited purchase leverage, credit history and export operating data.
  • Large commodity shipments can tie up cash before payment is received.
  • Quality capabilities depend on supplier and third-party processor discipline until the business has its own systems.
  • Specialty products have small addressable volumes and require strong provenance controls.

Opportunities

  • Convert market interest into repeat contracts through structured samples, buyer visits and supplier qualification.
  • Offer graded cashews and specified cassava flour/starch products instead of undifferentiated spot trading.
  • Capture premium positioning for authentic Kampot Pepper with GI-linked documentation.
  • Explore additional China-approved specialty-product routes as official market access expands.

Threats

  • Competing supply from Thailand, Vietnam and established cashew processing/export hubs.
  • Commodity price swings, seasonality, moisture damage, contaminants and rejected lots.
  • Rule changes, non-eligible product/plant combinations, customs reclassification or changing documentation requirements.
  • Buyer payment delay, currency movement, shipping disruption and supplier fraud.

Market-entry conclusions

Recommended go-to-market sequenceWin repeatable orders for dried cassava chips and eligible cashew products first; validate cassava flour under the exact Chinese product classification; build Kampot Pepper as a small-volume provenance-led line; keep bird's nest outside the base-case forecast until the specific approved source and route are contractually and regulatorily confirmed.

Commercial policy — minimum rules

Pricing policy

Every quote should show product/grade, unit basis, quantity tier, Incoterm and named location, freight validity, quality assumptions, payment terms, tax/duty responsibility and quote expiry. Track contribution after all variable costs—not just the buy-sell spread.

Credit policy

New buyers: prepayment, deposit + balance against documents, LC at sight or another approved secured structure. Any open-account term requires credit checks, limit, approval, aging review and stop-ship threshold.

Contract protection

Specify quality method, independent inspector, weight basis, tolerance, title/risk transfer, loading/delivery window, delay consequences, force majeure, claims evidence, dispute resolution and governing law reviewed by counsel.

Inventory and logistics policy

Buy-to-order first

Purchase against confirmed orders where practical. Limit speculative inventory to approved thresholds, especially during seasonal peaks or when the buyer's specification/market access is unresolved.

Condition & preservation

Define storage conditions and allowable moisture/temperature exposure per commodity. Keep lots segregated and protected from rain, pests, odour, contamination and package damage. Record warehouse entry and dispatch weights.

Shipment visibility

Use shipment milestones: booking, cargo ready, inspection passed, customs released, loaded/sealed, departure, arrival, clearance, delivered and paid. Escalate exceptions early to buyer and carrier.

06 • Regulatory readiness

Export Compliance & China Market Access

Regulatory eligibility is product-, processing-form-, origin- and establishment-specific. This page is a control checklist, not a legal opinion; the competent authority and China importer/broker must confirm requirements before shipment.

Pre-shipment gate
Do not ship on the basis of a general statement that “Cambodia can export this product.”Verify the exact commodity description, HS code, country/region access, establishment listing, quarantine protocol, registration route, labels, inspections and import permissions for the specific product and supplier. China issued new overseas food-manufacturer registration rules under GACC Decree 280 effective 1 June 2026, replacing Decree 248; do not rely solely on legacy Decree 248 guides.

Useful primary references

GACC Decree 280 (effective 1 June 2026): China's updated framework classifies overseas food manufacturers by risk and category; some food categories require recommendation by the competent authority in the exporting country. Confirm the current route for each SKU.

China–Cambodia FTA: the official China FTA portal publishes tariff schedules, product-specific rules and origin certificate annexes. Preferential treatment is conditional on the product meeting the rules of origin and documentation requirements.

Kampot Pepper GI: FAO documents the GI framework and the production zone in Kampot and Kep; only legitimate eligible GI-linked operators should supply product sold under the GI identity.

Bird's nest: use the latest competent-authority/GACC listing. Public reporting of one authorised exporter is not a standing approval for every Cambodian producer.

Incident response playbook

Product quality incident

  1. Place the affected lot on hold and block dispatch.
  2. Preserve samples, COA, photos and chain-of-custody records.
  3. Notify management, buyer and appropriate authorities where required.
  4. Investigate root cause, agree disposition and document corrective action before release.

Late or missing payment

  1. Stop new exposure if terms/limit are breached.
  2. Reconcile documents, delivery evidence, invoice and disputes.
  3. Escalate according to the contract; activate collection and legal process if needed.
  4. Update buyer credit limit and cash forecast.

Customs / market access issue

  1. Do not improvise or alter product declarations to force clearance.
  2. Ask the importer/broker and competent authority for the formal reason and required remedy.
  3. Hold related lots; assess detention, demurrage, return or destruction costs.
  4. Update the product gate checklist before any future shipment.
08 • Execution plan

Implementation Roadmap

Launch in controlled phases. The priority is to prove buyer demand, route eligibility, quality repeatability and cash conversion before increasing volume or fixed assets.

First 24 months + scale-up
Phase 1 • 0–30 days

Foundation & controls

  • Confirm business registration, tax, bank, signing authority and trade scope.
  • Choose priority 2–3 SKUs and define product/grade specifications.
  • Shortlist 2–3 qualified suppliers per core product.
  • Map HS, import eligibility, certificate and origin requirements.
  • Build supplier, buyer, shipment and cash-control templates.
Phase 2 • 31–90 days

Validate market fit

  • Interview 20–30 target Chinese buyer accounts.
  • Collect written specifications, target price and monthly demand.
  • Run samples/tests and secure buyer sample approvals.
  • Negotiate pilot contracts and payment security.
  • Complete test shipments only where regulatory gate is clear.
Phase 3 • Months 4–12

Repeatable fulfilment

  • Convert pilots to repeat orders and framework contracts.
  • Track quality claims, delivery performance and contribution.
  • Develop supplier scorecards and alternate-source plans.
  • Complete monthly P&L, margin and 13-week cash review.
  • Validate premium Kampot Pepper sourcing; keep bird's nest gated.
Phase 4 • Years 2–5

Scale selectively

  • Expand only when current buyers absorb repeat volume.
  • Add processing, packaging or warehouse assets only with utilisation case.
  • Develop differentiated grades/brands and additional qualified accounts.
  • Renegotiate logistics based on data and consolidate shipments.
  • Explore new products after route, supplier and margin validation.

Working-capital design

The working-capital estimate uses: receivables + inventory − supplier payables + safety reserve. It is intentionally simplified and does not replace an invoice-level cash forecast.

Receivables days, inventory days and payable days can be edited below. Long collection terms, speculative stock, border delay or buyer disputes can create a cash requirement far above this estimate.

Control: maintain a rolling 13-week cash forecast; update it at least weekly while shipping; compare required working capital with cleared cash and committed credit before accepting each order.

Trade-finance priorityNegotiate deposits or documentary payment terms so cash timing is not materially worse than the supplier payment schedule. Treat unapproved open-account exposure as a financing decision—not a sales convenience.
